Does your event need a liquor licence?

In Nova Scotia, serving or selling alcohol at an event takes a licence. If your venue holds its own liquor licence, like a restaurant, hotel or licensed hall, it usually runs the bar under that licence. Ask the venue first.

If the venue isn't licensed, you need a Special Occasion Liquor Licence from Service Nova Scotia for the day. People often call it a special event licence. That covers most of the places events actually happen: offices, coworking spaces, galleries, museums, warehouses, barns, farms, community halls and church halls.

The rules that come with the licence

A special occasion licence isn't just a fee. The person who signs the application takes responsibility for how the event runs. These are the conditions that matter most:

  • Alcohol has to come from a legal source: the NSLC, an NSLC agency store, a winery or a microbrewery. Homemade and donated alcohol aren't allowed. This is why licensed events can't be bring-your-own.
  • Servers need responsible service training. Anyone pouring must have completed an approved responsible alcohol service program. In Nova Scotia, that's Serve Right.
  • Someone responsible is on site. The application names a person, 19 or older, who will be present and responsible for the event.
  • The venue has said yes. You need permission to hold the event there, and written authorization may be requested.
  • Fire safety and capacity. The licence holder is responsible for following fire safety measures and the occupant load. A letter from a fire official may be required.
  • Inspectors can drop in. Service Nova Scotia staff may enter and inspect the event at any time.

Outdoor events need more

Tents, patios, yards and festival sites take extra documents on top of the application:

  • An enclosed licensed area: a tent, canopy, patio or a fence at least 4 feet high
  • A site plan showing the licensed area's dimensions, enclosures and washroom locations
  • Fire official approval with occupancy. In HRM, that's Halifax Fire Prevention
  • Security details, including the security company, number of staff and the signed agreement
  • In HRM, a letter from Halifax Regional Police confirming they're aware of the event

None of it is hard, but it takes time to collect. If your bar is going outside, start earlier.

When to apply

  1. Book your bar first. The application needs your date, venue, hours and expected attendance.
  2. At least 10 days out. The regulations require the application at least 10 days before the event, and Service Nova Scotia asks for at least 10 business days to process it. In practice, that's two weeks or more.
  3. Earlier for outdoor events. Fire approval, site plans and security agreements all have to be in hand before you submit.
